## Overview

The 2,000 lbf RP-1/LOX [regeneratively cooled engine](/Portfolio/projects/regeneratively-cooled-engine/) is gimbaled for thrust vector control by two linear actuators mounted 90° apart, one per axis. I worked the full sizing chain: requirements, kinematics, gimbal torque, actuator selection, a clearance check of the mount layout against the chamber, and margins of safety on every joint in the load path.

All of it lives in one spreadsheet. The Inputs tab holds the mount points, requirements and load assumptions, and every other tab (geometry, loads, validation, joint checks, CAD dimensions) is a formula off those inputs. Moving a pin or swapping an actuator re-runs the whole chain, which is what made the layout iterations below cheap.

## Kinematics

The model is planar, one gimbal axis at a time, with the origin at the gimbal pivot. The actuator's rear pin A is fixed to the thrust structure and its front pin P rides on the chamber, so gimbaling the chamber by θ rotates P about the pivot:

- P(θ) = (P<sub>x</sub> cos θ − P<sub>y</sub> sin θ, P<sub>x</sub> sin θ + P<sub>y</sub> cos θ)
- L(θ) = |P(θ) − A|, the pin-to-pin length
- dL/dθ = (A<sub>x</sub>P<sub>y</sub>(θ) − A<sub>y</sub>P<sub>x</sub>(θ)) / L, the actuator's moment arm
- v = |dL/dθ| ω and a = |d²L/dθ²| ω² + |dL/dθ| α for actuator speed and acceleration

With the final pins at A = (250, 100) mm and P = (95.9, −200) mm, the actuator uses 61.6 mm of stroke over ±10°. The moment arm is smallest at −10° (161.8 mm/rad), and that is the angle that sets the force. Peak speed is 66.3 mm/s and peak acceleration 108 mm/s².

## Gimbal torque and actuator force

Running torque at ±10° builds up from six terms. The total is 114.7 N·m, and the biggest single term is the one I know least about: the propellant hoses, which are a 50 N·m placeholder until we have line specs or a bend test.

<figure class="fig fig--chart"><img src="/Portfolio/images/tvc/tvc-torque.svg" alt="Running torque bars: hoses 50.0, angular misalignment 21.7, bearing friction 16.9, offset misalignment 13.3, pendulum 12.5, inertia 0.2 newton-meters" loading="lazy" /></figure>

Holding torque at startup is driven by a 10% side load at the nozzle exit: 336.9 N·m from the side load plus 35.1 N·m of misalignment, 371.9 N·m in total. Friction is left out of holding because it resists motion, so leaving it out is conservative. Divided by the moment arm and multiplied by the 1.5 load margin, each actuator needs:

- **1.06 kN** running force (worst case at −10°, smallest arm)
- **3.16 kN** holding force at startup
- **66.3 mm/s** speed

## Actuator selection

I screened 19 Tolomatic IMA configurations against those numbers. Force wasn't what eliminated most of them: every 3-stack and larger unit has a 152.4 mm minimum stroke, and this layout can only use about 84 mm before the actuator runs out of length between the pins. That left the three single-stack IMA33 variants, and I picked the RN04 because it has the most force of the three.

| IMA33-RN04 (1-stack) | Required | Rated | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous force | 1,063 N | 2,000 N | +0.88 |
| Peak force | 3,158 N | 6,010 N | +0.90 |
| Speed | 66.3 mm/s | 244 mm/s | 3.7× |
| Stroke to order | 80.4 mm | 76.2–457.2 mm catalog | fits |
| Body width / mass | | 83.6 mm / 6.1 kg | |

The holding check uses peak force as a stand-in. IMA actuators are backdrivable, so the startup hit needs either the brake option or a holding rating confirmed with Tolomatic.

## Layout: from hand calc to final mounts

The first pass was by hand, in the "Original work" tab: an actuator mount at (101.5, 100) and a chamber attach point at (75, −150), with the lengths checked at neutral and ±10° by plotting the points.

<figure class="fig"><img src="/Portfolio/images/tvc-first-layout.jpg" alt="Hand-calculation geometry: gimbal at the origin, actuator mount at 101.5, 100, neutral chamber attach at 75, -150 and the tilted attach point at 47.8, -160.7, with actuator lengths at neutral and at -10 degrees" loading="lazy" /></figure>

That got the method working. Clearance was the next problem: an earlier layout touched the injector flange at −5.6° and overlapped it by 5.9 mm at −10°. Checking clearance properly meant rotating every chamber profile point by θ, measuring its distance to the actuator body (or to the rod, past the body), and subtracting half the body width and a 3 mm minimum gap. Anything negative is interference.

The final layout puts the rear pin at (250, 100) and the chamber pin at (95.9, −200), which stands the actuator up and keeps its body clear of the flange across the whole range. The spreadsheet sweeps the angle in 0.25° steps out to 30° each way and records the first constraint that fails:

- **+10.75°**, limited by the actuator reaching full retraction
- **−12.0°**, limited by full extension
- **25.4 mm** of clearance margin at neutral, 12.4 mm at −10°
- **291.8 mm** outermost point, against the 350 mm envelope

<figure class="fig fig--chart"><img src="/Portfolio/images/tvc/tvc-clearance.svg" alt="Clearance margin to the chamber rises linearly from about 6 mm at -15 degrees to 42 mm at +15; 12.4 mm at -10, 25.4 at neutral, 37.9 at +10; shaded regions beyond +10.75 and -12 degrees where the actuator runs out of travel" loading="lazy" /></figure>

Clearance never runs out inside the travel window. The angle limit is the actuator's own stroke, which is the better failure mode: it is a hard stop I can see in the catalog, not a collision.

## Joint design

The joints are sized to what the actuator can physically push, not just the predicted load: 1.5 × the actuator's 6,010 N peak, 9,015 N in total, split 4,508 N into each chamber lug. That way the joints stay below yield even if the controller drives into a hard stop at full current. Load path: printed GRCop-42 chamber lugs → Ø16 mm front pin → Tolomatic SRE spherical rod end → actuator → Ø12 mm rear pin → 6061-T6 tongue on the thrust structure.

<figure class="fig"><img src="/Portfolio/images/tvc-clevis-joint.jpg" alt="Front joint diagram: structural ring on the chamber, clevis bracket with two lugs, pin in double shear, ball swiveling on the pin, rod end threaded shank, jam nut, actuator rod and actuator body" loading="lazy" /></figure>

<figure class="fig fig--chart"><img src="/Portfolio/images/tvc/tvc-margins.svg" alt="Margins of safety, lowest first: rod end tilt +0.00, thread engagement +0.85, actuator continuous +0.88, actuator peak +0.90, tongue shear-out +1.39, rod end radial rating +2.22, lug shear-out +3.00, lug root bending +3.04, tongue net section +3.14, tongue bearing +3.97, lug bearing +4.62, front pin bending +4.70, lug net section +5.20, shank tension +5.54, rear pin shear +8.48, front pin shear +15.86" loading="lazy" /></figure>

The chamber lugs are checked at a hot yield of 130.2 MPa (186 MPa room-temperature GRCop-42 × a 0.7 temperature knockdown). The lowest margins sit at the rod end: its 10° tilt rating exactly matches the 10° gimbal angle, and its thread engagement. Lug root bending (*) is before the fillet's stress concentration, so it still needs FEA.

## Open items

- **Lug edge distance.** e/d is 1.06, below the 1.5 where the simple lug formulas hold, so the lug checks are optimistic until FEA or Bruhn's lug-efficiency curves confirm them.
- **Rod end tilt.** The 10° tilt rating has zero margin against the 10° gimbal angle. The catalog tab already carries a Fluro GAXS12x1.25 with 13° of tilt and a 42 kN rating as the fallback.
- **Lug temperature.** The 0.7 GRCop-42 knockdown is a placeholder until the thermal analysis gives a lug temperature.
- **Hose torque.** 44% of the running torque and still an estimate, so it is the most valuable number to measure.
- **Holding.** Spec the actuator brake, or confirm a holding rating with Tolomatic.
- **Both axes at once.** Every check is per axis. With both axes at full deflection the chamber tilts about 1.41× further, so the nozzle swing needs a 3D check at that angle.
